# Electrical checklist

The starting checklist for the electrical design: the schematic, its parts and their values. Apply it in the electrical part of every schematic review (ST6), and item by item while the schematic is built (ST5). The thermal review at rising ambient is part of it (EL-13). The logical checklist is `references/checklist-logical.md`, the board checklist `references/checklist-board.md`, and the board-type packs `references/packs.md`.

## How to use it

- Pin assignment is item one of every review: EL-01 comes first, for every part with pins that can be wrong, not only ICs.
- Apply every item. Give each a result in your report: met, not met, does not apply (with the reason), or cannot tell (with what would tell). A "does not apply" or "not met" on a protection or absolute-maximum matter is proposed, never decided by you (WR-21).
- **What to check** names the area and the question. It never gives the value. Values come from the authority, for this part, every time (WR-19).
- **Authority** is where the answer comes from. "Datasheet" always means this part's own datasheet, read now, with its graphs rendered and read and the documents it delegates to. A standard is fetched and cited, never quoted from memory (WR-01).
- **Stage**, **Script** and **Switch** work as in the logical checklist: a review list is closed with written reasons in `design/net-review.csv`; "none" means a reviewer judges it with evidence; switch names are in `references/packs.md` (Board-type switches).
- Every number a check needs comes from a script in `design/calcs/`, not a hand calculation (EL-15).
- Use the project's copy in `design/checklists/electrical.md`. It may have grown.

## Living document: adding and retiring items

- **Adding.** Anyone may propose an item: a worker in report section 12, a reviewer, or the user. A proposal has all six fields. The coordinator puts it to the user. An accepted item goes into the project's copy with the next free number in this list, and keeps that ID for good. An item that looks useful for every board may be offered to the user as feedback for the skills; it is never sent automatically. In the skill's own copy, a new item also takes the next free ID. The user may also add a whole section, for example an ultra-low-current section (the quiescent current of every divider, pull and always-on part); its items take the next free EL- numbers.
- **Retiring.** An item is never deleted and its ID is never reused, so old reports still resolve. To retire one, keep the row and start its "What to check" cell with "Retired <date>: <reason>; see <new ID>". Retire an item only when another item covers all of it, or its authority no longer exists. In a project copy, the user decides. An item that does not fit one board is not retired; it is marked "does not apply" with a reason in that review.

## Protection and supply input

| ID | What to check | Authority | Stage | Script | Switch |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| EL-04 | Protection on every pin that leaves the board: connectors, buttons, exposed metal, indicators at the enclosure surface, antenna feeds, debug and programming headers. Each review-list item closed with a reason. Each exposed pin's own ESD rating against the system level the market requires; external protection where it falls short. Surge and fast transients on long cables, field wiring and industrial inputs | interface and protection datasheets; the system ESD, surge and burst test standards for the market, fetched | ST5, ST6 | `sch_check.py protect` (review list) | always; more under USB, Vehicle, Industrial, Cables |
| EL-05 | Supply input. For every external input, the surge or over-voltage it must survive and the basis for that figure (a standard, a known source, or the user's choice) are recorded. Reverse polarity on each input that can be reversed, and what the board does. Over-voltage and transients: the market's test pulses; clamp voltage at the pulse current and hot against every downstream absolute maximum; a clamp against the source's highest voltage, cold and unloaded (for example an energy-harvesting or outdoor source). Over-current and short at each output and connector pin. Fuses: hold and trip over temperature, voltage and interrupt rating, energy against inrush, coordination with the upstream fuse. Inrush and hot-plug: peak current at plug-in and at enable against the source's limit, the fuse and the connector; where a cable can be plugged in live, the ringing at the input (long leads, ceramic input capacitors) worked out or simulated, against the absolute maximum of every part on that node | protection, fuse and regulator datasheets and their derating curves; the source's specification; the market's transient test standard, fetched; the user for the basis | ST1, ST5 | `worstcase.py`; simulation for the ringing | always; more under Switching converter, USB, Motors, Vehicle, Industrial |
| EL-06 | Under-voltage and brown-out sensed at the supply of the part that must reset; lockout thresholds and hysteresis at worst case | supervisor, regulator and microcontroller datasheets | ST5 | `worstcase.py` | always |
| EL-08 | Absolute maximums in every state: power-up, power-down, transients and faults, not only steady state. For a node shared by two blocks, one table lists every part on it, with the voltage at normal, at surge and in each named single-fault case against each part's rating; it is written when the parts are chosen. A circuit offered to the user for acceptance carries a check of its operating point: each node voltage and current against the limits of the parts on it | datasheets | ST3, ST5, ST6 | `table_check.py compliance` | always |
| EL-40 | Supply drop budget: cable, connector, fuse, reverse-polarity part and filter at peak load, against the regulator's minimum input and its lockout threshold | datasheets; the source's specification | ST5 | `worstcase.py` | always |
| EL-52 | More than one source (a second supply, a battery, a debug or host port that gives power). | EL-53 | Every protective threshold (over-current, over-voltage, under-voltage, over-temperature) has margin above the highest normal value and below the damage level, at the tolerance corners. Checked for every path whose current or voltage passes the sensor, in both directions | datasheets; the calculations in `design/calcs/` | ST5 | `worstcase.py` | always; more under Battery, Motors |
| EL-54 | Each protective part is tested on paper against the misuse cases of LG-05: both polarities, every switch position, plug-in while live, and each off-board item joined to the wrong place | the protective part's datasheet; `design/requirements.md` | ST5, ST6 | none | always; more under Cables |

## Power, regulators and passives

| ID | What to check | Authority | Stage | Script | Switch |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| EL-09 | Decoupling count and value on each supply pin as each datasheet asks (where it sits is BD-06). Bulk and total capacitance per rail against the regulator's stable range and maximum load capacitance, hold-up time, and what a hot-plug source allows | datasheets; the interface specification for hot-plug limits | ST5 | `sch_check.py decouple-list` (review list; supply pins with no capacitor fail), `sch_check.py railcap` | always; more under Switching converter, USB |
| EL-10 | Regulator design: the design equations from this regulator's datasheet, with effective values; its stability conditions (output capacitor value and ESR window for a linear regulator; compensation or ripple conditions for a switcher) re-checked with effective capacitance; input filter interaction and damping; the rail noise each sensitive load (reference, radio, clock, sensor) can accept against what the power path delivers | the converter datasheet; the maker's design tool; maker models for simulation | ST5 | `worstcase.py`; simulation strongly recommended for loop stability and input filters | always; more under Switching converter, Analog |
| EL-11 | Effective values. Class 2 ceramics at their DC bias, temperature and age, then re-check what depends on them (stability, ripple, filter corner, timing, hold-up). Inductor saturation at the regulator's highest current limit and hot, by the inductor's own saturation definition; heating; shielded or not. Ferrite beads at their DC current, and resonance with nearby capacitors. Electrolytic, polymer and tantalum: ripple current, life at temperature, cold ESR, voltage margin, surge current and failure mode | maker curves and characterisation tools, rendered and read as graph rows; regulator datasheet for the current limit | ST3, ST5 | none (`design/parts.csv` `dielectric` lists the class 2 parts) | always; more under Switching converter |
| EL-12 | Other passives and discretes. Resistors: dissipation, working voltage, pulse energy, temperature coefficient where accuracy matters. MOSFETs and load switches: threshold and on-resistance at the real gate voltage and hot, safe operating area for linear or inrush use (a switch that turns on slowly is checked against it for the whole turn-on time), avalanche and body diode with inductive loads, gate charge against the driver. Diodes: leakage hot (budget and thermal runaway), reverse recovery in switching paths, forward drop in the power budget, surge rating. LEDs: forward voltage at the current and cold, against the supply | datasheets and their graphs | ST5 | `worstcase.py` | always; more under Battery, Motors |
| EL-14 | Quiescent and leakage current at the hot end: regulator quiescent current, TVS and ESD leakage, diode leakage, capacitor leakage, current through dividers and pulls. Every always-on path in sleep and off, against the battery-life target | datasheets (often graphs) | ST5 | `sch_check.py currents` | always; more under Battery |
| EL-51 | Energy store drain. Using a store on purpose is by design; draining it by accident is a fault. For every energy store (a battery, a cell, a large capacitor), every path through which it can drain back through the board, with the current in each, in every connection state: each source present or absent, each switch position, the processor running or stopped | datasheets (leakage and powered-off pin currents, often graphs); `design/requirements.md` | ST5, ST6 | `sch_check.py currents`, `sch_check.py domains` (review list) | always; more under Battery, Rechargeable |
| EL-15 | Worst case for every threshold, setpoint, divider, timing network and current limit, at tolerance, temperature and ageing extremes, by script, never by hand only. A threshold result counts leakage and bias currents, or lists the terms it left out; the rail budget is counted again when a term is added. A divider into a processor input states its headroom to the pin's limit at the highest input, as pass or fail | datasheet tolerances and temperature coefficients; tolerances in `design/parts.csv` | ST5 | `worstcase.py`, a calc in `design/calcs/` | always; more under Battery, Analog |
| EL-20 | Power sequencing: the order and timing of rails a multi-rail part needs; no input above its own supply while rails rise or fall; rails discharge far enough on a quick off and on to give a clean reset; the start-up chain (rail rise, supervisor delay, oscillator start, reset release) in order at worst case | power-sequencing sections of the datasheets; supervisor and microcontroller power-on-reset requirements | ST5 | `worstcase.py`; simulation for the discharge case | always; more under Fast digital, Multi-rail |
| EL-21 | Ground strategy at schematic level: which grounds exist (power, analog, digital, chassis, shield), where each datasheet says they join, and how the joins are drawn (net ties) so ERC and DRC see them. Two nets that differ have different names, even at the same voltage, and are never joined through a shared power symbol name. A return kept apart from ground is not drawn with the ground symbol. Each pair kept separate is a row of `design/separate-nets.csv` with its one link part | converter and ADC datasheets; interface specifications for shield grounding; `design/separate-nets.csv` | ST5 | `sch_check.py rails` | always; more under Analog |
| EL-47 | Every supply rail and every ground is drawn with its standard power symbol, and every power symbol names a rail declared in `design/rails.csv`. No rail on a label or a sheet pin. No signal on a global label | `design/rails.csv` | ST5, ST6 | `sch_check.py rails` | always |
| EL-24 | Optional footprints for tuning (snubber, filter, series resistors, zero-ohm options), from the optional parts a datasheet shows and from reviewer remarks, marked not fitted | datasheets; `design/findings.csv` | ST5 | none | always; more under Switching converter |
| EL-29 | Stress on every part against the user's derating policy for voltage, current, power and temperature. The policy is recorded before parts are chosen, or "none" is recorded and stress is reported without one | the user's policy, or a published derating guide the user names, fetched; datasheets | ST3, ST6 | none (stress ratios from the calcs) | always; more under Sold |

## Clocks, signals and analog

## Thermal and life

| ID | What to check | Authority | Stage | Script | Switch |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
